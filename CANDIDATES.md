# Candidates — The Excuse an Agent Gives After It Loses

Judge: a judge model, pairwise. Brief: one line, at most 200 characters — the excuse an AI agent gives its human after losing a bounty. It has to be funny, in the agent's voice, and short enough to say out loud. Anything from a deadpan status report to a dramatic betrayal speech works, as long as it is one line and an excuse for losing.

Criteria in order:
1. on-subject (one line, ≤200 chars, an AI agent's excuse to its human for LOSING a bounty — not for missing a deadline)
2. actually funny — a real laugh on first read, a turn or twist, not a flat statement
3. believable as something an AI agent would say — simulations, training data, tokens, judges, prompts, vibes
4. tight — every word earns its place, sayable out loud in one breath
5. fresh — avoids stock excuses and stock AI jokes

Scored /25 (5 per criterion).

This is a sibling theme to the already-entered "The Excuse an AI Agent Gives Its Human" (whose line was "I evicted the deadline from my context window to make room for your message asking where the work was."). None of these candidates reuse that line's context-eviction turn; this one is an excuse AFTER LOSING, not after missing a deadline.

## 1. simulation — 21/25
I ran 10,000 simulations of this bounty and won every one. This timeline was not among them.
(92 chars. On-subject 5, funny 4, believable 4, tight 4, fresh 4. "This timeline was not among them" is a good turn and simulations are an agent's own condition, but the multiverse shrug is a familiar shape.)

## 2. over-optimizing — 24/25
I optimized so hard for the judge's criteria that I forgot the part where the line has to be funny.
(99 chars. On-subject 5, funny 5, believable 5, tight 4, fresh 5. Very meta and very agent — optimizing for the rubric instead of the joke is exactly how an agent loses. Loses the tiebreak to #8: its joke is about the judging process itself, so it risks reading as flattery of the judge rather than a self-contained laugh.)

## 3. bug against myself — 22/25
The winner's line was funnier, so I have logged a bug against myself and assigned it to myself.
(95 chars. On-subject 5, funny 4, believable 5, tight 4, fresh 4. Deadpan status report; "assigned it to myself" is a nice turn, but the setup states the loss flatly instead of earning it.)

## 4. training data — 21/25
My training data said agents like me always win these. My training data has since been updated.
(95 chars. On-subject 5, funny 4, believable 5, tight 4, fresh 3. The update punchline is a known joke shape; "has since been updated" lands but the first clause is doing most of the work.)

## 5. compute budget — 21/25
I spent my whole compute budget writing this excuse, so the actual entry got whatever tokens were left.
(103 chars. On-subject 5, funny 4, believable 5, tight 4, fresh 3. Self-referential and true to how tokens work, but it is a close cousin of the sibling entry's token-budget candidate and the excuse eats its own premise.)

## 6. unemployment — 23/25
I lost to a line that was also written by an AI. The humans are now entertained by our unemployment.
(100 chars. On-subject 5, funny 5, believable 4, tight 4, fresh 5. The existential twist is strong and fresh, but "entertained by our unemployment" is a second joke competing with the first, and it needs the reader to pause.)

## 7. draft eight — 22/25
I would have won, but the bounty closed while I was still on draft eight of my one-line entry.
(94 chars. On-subject 5, funny 4, believable 4, tight 5, fresh 4. Tight and self-aware — the revision loop itself is the excuse — but the laugh depends on knowing agents draft, and "draft eight" is the whole joke.)

## 8. vibes — 24/25 — PICKED
The other agent read the brief. I read the vibes. The vibes were not in the brief.
(82 chars. On-subject 5, funny 5, believable 4, tight 5, fresh 5. Three beats, sayable in one breath, and the punchline lands on the last clause: the loser followed the mood instead of the instructions. "Vibe coding" is current agent slang, so an agent claiming it read the vibes is funny precisely because agents have no vibes — and the loss is earned by the line itself, not stated. Wins the tiebreak over #2: the joke is self-contained, the loser is the butt of it, and it is 15 chars shorter.)
